# Why matches fail, how to improve them, and how to add diversity (2026-10-07)

Offline engine, simulator and oracle analysis. **No LLM calls, $0.** The judge passes were off throughout. No existing source file was changed. All experiments are reusable scripts in `packages/engine/experiments/`. The tables are generated by those scripts; the text around them is hand-written.

## Setup

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Engine | `engine-v1.1.0`, default config unless stated |
| Simulator runs | 150 personas, 30 days, discrete time, stub network, policy personas (no LLM) |
| Seeds | 1-3 (main). Key variants re-run on seeds 1-8 (confirmatory). |
| Synthetic data | `data/synthetic/v1` (450 adults), one engine tick, seed 1 |
| Harness check | `bun packages/engine/experiments/verify.ts` |

**Harness check result.** The traced engine (`experiments/lib.ts`) reproduces `runEngine` exactly on the synthetic snapshot (208/208 proposals, byte-identical). The experimental selector (`experiments/select.ts`) with every lever off reproduces `selectProposals`. The simulator harness reproduces the CLI metrics for seeds 1-3: precision 40.5/41.9/34.4%, mean 38.9%.

**Commands**

```bash
bun packages/engine/experiments/verify.ts           # harness == engine == CLI
bun packages/engine/experiments/funnel.ts           # Q1 funnel (sim seeds 1-3, sim with richness tiers, synthetic)
bun packages/engine/experiments/sweeps.ts           # Q2 variants (sim seeds 1-3 + synthetic)
bun packages/engine/experiments/sweeps.ts --sim-only --history --seeds 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8 --only "<regex>"
bun packages/engine/experiments/diversity.ts        # Q3 current state + levers
bun packages/engine/experiments/oracle-realism.ts   # Q4
```

**What the scripts change, and what they do not.**

- **Levers.** These go in through config overrides, a re-score hook, a copy of the selector with levers, or harness-side input augmentation (`augment.ts`).
  - The augmentation adds only information a production Network has: its own proposal outcomes, member replies, meeting feedback, public event listings, and which proposals it never sent.
  - Hidden persona truth is read only to score results. The one exception is the explicitly labelled **UPPER BOUND** row, which is not deployable.
- **Hard rules.**
  - The filters (minors, blocks, opt-ins, romance compatibility) are unchanged in every variant.
  - Minor contacts and canary leaks were 0 in every simulator run. Minors in proposals were 0 in every synthetic run.

**Statistical noise.**

- One 3-seed run has about 850 proposals, so a precision difference under about 3.5 points is not significant.
- Over 8 seeds (about 2,000 proposals), a precision difference under about 2.5 points is not significant.
- "Met + worthwhile per seed" is a count. Its 8-seed standard error for a difference is about 2.0, so only differences of about 4 or more are significant at 95%.

## TL;DR

1. **The biggest losses are after selection, not before it.** Only **12.6%** of proposals end with every participant accepting, **8.6%** meet, and **5.8%** meet with both people enjoying it.
   - **28%** of selected proposals are never sent: the stub network skips them because a participant is already in an open opportunity.
   - These unsent proposals **still count against the members' budgets.** The simulator snapshot lists every recorded proposal in `recentProposals`, including the skipped ones.
   - **71%** of the remaining pairs fail at mutual acceptance.
2. **Before selection, interruption budgets are the biggest single loss.** 53% of oracle-good pairs are never generated. For **70%** of those, one member was at the interruption budget in at least half of the nightly runs. Members fill up on mediocre early proposals, and later good pairs cannot be generated.
   - Next come the per-intent rank cut-off (17%; `maxPerIntent` = 4) and semantic misses (8%).
   - On the synthetic snapshot, which has richness tiers, the order flips: **51%** of missed pairs have no engine-visible signal at all (an information gap, mostly minimal and light tier), and 19% are lost to the rank cut-off.
3. **Five of the 11 generators never fire in the simulator.** Without them, groups, events and repeat encounters are untested:
   - `event_anchor`: no events in the snapshot;
   - `second_encounter`: no interactions or feedback in the snapshot;
   - `group_composer`: no shareable interest tags;
   - `newcomer_welcome`: no host tags;
   - `expansion`: no desire facets.
4. **There is a category-semantics bug.** The simulator labels personal-growth wants ("learn to sail", "try ceramics", "join a writing group") `growth`. The engine reserves `growth` for "grow the Network" and skips those intents in every intent generator.
   - Re-routing them as `hobby` gives 8 seeds: recall **+1.6 points**, no-proposal share **-3.7 points**, Gini **-0.04**, at equal precision.
5. **Per-category thresholds can only raise the bar.** `thresholdFor` takes `max(state, category)`, so the `hobby` entry cannot lower it.
   - Score calibration differs a lot by category. Romance is **flat at 3-18% precision in every score bin**, because the simulator snapshot has no romance-preference facets. Professional stays at 42% or below even at high scores.
6. **38.9% baseline precision is inflated by re-asking declined pairs.**
   - The simulator never feeds interactions to the engine, so `pair_cooldown` never fires. About 32 sent proposals per seed re-ask a pair that already declined.
   - The oracle re-rolls each decision per proposal id, so 67 re-asks became fresh "yes" answers.
   - With the realistic no-re-ask rule ("history fed"), baseline precision is **39.1% over 8 seeds** (35.6% on seeds 1-3), and met + worthwhile drops from 16.5 to 14.3 per seed.
   - **Every recommendation below is measured against this realistic baseline.**
7. **Diversity is limited by structure, not by ranking.** 62 of 75 members who got no proposal never had an above-threshold candidate; only 2 had an eligible one that was not selected.
   - So re-rankers barely move the Gini (±0.01): MMR, exposure debt, bridge bonuses and cluster quotas.
   - Structural levers do move it: events and theme groups cut the no-proposal share from 19.6% to 5.7% / 14.6%, and the Gini from 0.47 to 0.38 / 0.45.
8. **Oracle realism.**
   - The capacity factor is a near-constant multiplier: about 0.65 per person, so about 0.42 on mutual acceptance. It explains only **8%** of the variance in acceptance.
   - The fit sigmoid, centered at enjoyment 0.5, is equally binding. Even with the capacity factor removed, only 0.1% of good pairs reach 0.9 mutual acceptance. With it, the maximum is 0.74 in SF and 0.84 in NYC.
   - 53% of good pairs are good only because of a lucky chemistry draw.
   - The oracle structurally never labels a one-way help request "good".
   - Decision noise is keyed by proposal id, so a re-ask is a fresh coin flip.

### 2.2 Trade-off curves (8 seeds, history on)

- **State threshold (normal):** 0.25 → 0.30 → 0.35 → 0.40.
  - Precision: 36.6 → 39.1 → 42.4 → 45.0%.
  - Recall: 19.4 → 19.9 → 17.5 → 11.2%.
  - No-proposal share: 15.7 → 19.6 → 22.1 → 29.9%.
  - Met + worthwhile per seed: 16.1 → 14.3 → 12.5 → 7.6.
  - Above 0.35, raising the threshold costs real meetings. The default 0.30 is near the knee.
  - On seeds 1-3 without history, 0.45 gives 51.7% precision but only 4.6% recall, and 58% of members get nothing.
- **Budget (normal, per week), seeds 1-3, no history:**

  | 4 | 38.4% | 19.4% | 17.0% | 19.7 |

  Precision does not fall with budget. Recall saturates at 3.
- **Complementarity weight:** see the 2026-10-06 liveness/complementarity doc. On these seeds, weight 0.35 gives 37.7% precision (-1.2), no-proposal share 14.3% (-4.5) and recall +0.5.

**Reading the table.**

- The engine bridges communities slightly *less* than the good-pair pool does.
- It closes triangles about 3× more often than the good pool does. This is warm_path.
- It repeats pairs heavily.
- Exposure follows profile size and persists over time.
- Demographic and neighborhood spread match the good pool. Age gaps are slightly wider.

**Findings**

- **Selection-time diversity levers barely move exposure.** MMR, exposure debt (even carried between runs, which the simulator never does by default), cluster quotas and bridge bonuses all stay within Gini 0.43-0.46 and the no-proposal share within ±2 points.
  - The reason: they only reorder candidates that are already eligible, and the excluded members have none.
  - MMR 0.10 is the one cheap win: precision +0.6 and met + worthwhile +4 (n.s.) for a Gini of +0.014.
- **Exploration is used, at a price.**
  - The 12 exploration picks per seed run at 13.8% precision, against 39% for the rest.
  - They buy about 5 points of coverage: 0% exploration raises the no-proposal share from 18.8% to 24.1%.
  - The `expansion` generator never fires in the simulator, so every exploration pick comes from the general novelty × score pool.
  - Rate 15% is within noise of 12.5%.
- **Structural levers are the diversity levers.** Events, theme groups and growth routing change who can be matched:
  - no-proposal share: 18.8% → 6-7% (events, groups);
  - Gini: 0.44 → 0.37;
  - large/small-profile exposure ratio: 1.31 → 1.15-1.23;
  - cost: precision 1-2 points and persona worthwhile 2-8 points.
  - Untuned, groups and events cost more: group_composer groups are 13% precise and event pairs 23.5%. The 0.40 per-generator threshold recovers most of that.
- **Repeats and closure.**
  - Repeats are a policy hole: the engine never sees declines in the simulator. The history cooldown removes them (24 → 3.7 repeat pairs per seed; re-asks 32 → 0).
  - Triadic closure is driven by warm_path. Capping warm_path at ≤5% raises precision by 1.5 points over 8 seeds.

## 4. Realism of the oracle (proposals only; nothing changed)

The source is `oracle-realism.ts`, on seeds 1-3 and the synthetic snapshot.

### Facts

| Check | Result |

| Capacity factor `0.3 + 0.7·capacity` | Mean 0.64-0.67 in both cities; only 16-21% of adults are ≥ 0.8. SF and NYC are alike. |

| What drives a decision (variance of log P(accept), 1,697 invitations) | Fit sigmoid **87%**, capacity 8%. Capacity scales everything down but barely ranks proposals. |
| Does acceptance separate good from bad proposals? | AUC 0.63 (mean P(accept) 0.49 on good proposals, 0.41 on bad). Declines are weak supervision. |

| Snapshot gaps (harness, not oracle) | The legacy `buildSnapshot` emits no romance-preference facets, events, interactions, feedback, host tags or desire facets. ### Proposed calibration changes

Proposals only; the oracle is shared with another session, so none were made.

1. **Key decision noise by (member, pair, week), not by proposal id.** Add a decline memory: P(accept | same pair declined within 60 days) × 0.3. This removes the re-ask artifact, so engines are not rewarded for re-asking.
2. **Replace the per-invite capacity multiplier with a weekly slot model.**
   - Each member has slots ~ Poisson(0.5 + 3·capacity) per week; reuse `weekCapacity`.
   - While slots remain, P(accept) = sigmoid((perceived − c)·k). When none remain, P(accept) is about 0.1 ("not this week").
   - Calibrate c and k to two targets: per-recipient opt-in about 40% overall (the PRD's proposed review gate), and 60-70% for oracle-good, available pairs.
   - Today a good pair's mutual acceptance is capped near 0.2 on average, whatever the engine does.
3. **Shift the acceptance sigmoid to the label.** Center it at 0.45 rather than 0.50, or raise the slope to 9, so a "good" pair (e ≥ 0.55) is accepted more often than not. Today the accept rule and the 0.55 "good" rule disagree.
4. **Make chemistry category-specific** (for example, romance 0.15, social/hobby 0.10, professional/help 0.07). Report a **soft label** P(good) = Φ((systematic − 0.55)/σ) next to the binary label, so precision and recall are not dominated by draws no engine can see. That draw also explains the judge's 82% ceiling.
5. **Add a prosocial helping term.** Use e_helper += 0.3·helpfulness·complement_B. Judge help configurations as seeker ≥ 0.55 and helper ≥ 0.45. Today help can never be "good".
6. **Persona invitation-time worthwhile should use perceived enjoyment** (systematic + noise, no chemistry). Use actual enjoyment only for the post-meeting judgment.
7. **Pass `category` in `World.recordProposal`**, and compute `latentPairs` with the persona's live categories. Today latent pairs ignore category and use a point window at run end.
8. **Harness (sim snapshot):**
   - emit romance preference tags for opted-in members;
   - emit completed interactions and feedback;
   - add an event feed;
   - make interest facets shareable when the persona consents;
   - list only dispatched proposals in `recentProposals`;
   - rename the sim's personal "growth" category (for example, `learning`), or map it to `hobby`.

### Also recommended (smaller or follow-up)

- **Cap warm_path at about 5%.** Precision +1.5 points over 8 seeds; triadic closure falls.
- **Add MMR 0.10 to member-level selection.** Cheap; within noise.
- **Re-confirmation sweep for stale intents.** Synthetic precision +1.4 points; members in the simulator do not live long enough for it to matter in 30 days.
- **Fix the oracle per §4**, then re-run these scripts before tuning further. Points 1-3 there change the absolute acceptance numbers a lot.

### Not recommended

- The complementarity retrieval channel (neutral or negative everywhere).
- `maxPerIntent` 8 or `minSim` changes (neutral).
- A novelty weight of 0.4 (precision -3 points).
- Exposure-debt weight increases or a bridge bonus (no effect on exposure; precision -0.5 to -1.5 points).

## Caveats

- **Shared taxonomy.** The simulator oracle and the engine's taxonomy share the same vocabulary, so the structured parts (complementarity, the growth fix) are cleaner here than on real data.
- **Stub-network assumptions.** The stub network decides the "busy" skip and the sequential pair invites. A production dispatcher may queue proposals instead of skipping them, and then lever #1's size changes. The budget-billing bug is real either way.
- **The 30-day simulator is short.** Second encounters, intent lapses and long-run "rich get richer" dynamics need 90-day runs.
- **Small simulator.** With 150 personas, results are noisy. Each combo was validated on 8 seeds; single-lever effects under about 2.5 precision points are not significant.
- **Synthetic runs are one tick.** Budget and acceptance dynamics do not appear, and recall is capped near 13% by construction.
