## Result in brief

slopPack is the slop.date AppPack on the shared engine. `runEngine(input, SLOP_ENGINE_CONFIG, { pack: slopPack })` runs the full pipeline: generators, hard filters, scoring, a pack assignment, asks and explanations. It passes the core conformance suite plus 18 slop-specific checks, and the networkPack goldens are unchanged (fast and full tier).

On the held-out seeds (5-8), against random-within-filters:

| | 4 weeks | 8 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Second-date rate | **12.7%** (1.5x random's 8.6%) | **15.6%** (2.2x random's 7.1%) |
| Second dates per seed | 15.5 (1.4x) | 34.8 (2.2x) |
| Dates per member-month | 1.03x random | 1.05x random |
| Stated-filter violations | 0 (random: 116 per seed) | 0 (random: 230) |
| Back-outs at the reveal | 11.0% (random 18.1%) | 10.6% (random 16.9%) |

Other held-out findings:
- **Congestion:** the top 10% of members get 14% of proposals (greedy: 41%).
- **Minors:** 0 declared-minor contacts. Contacts with lying minors fall from 5.0 to 3.0 per seed; with the PRD 40.5 verification step modelled, to 0.5.

**Gates.** The pack does not pass every proposed gate, and the reasons are structural rather than tuning:

1. **Second-date rate ≥ 2.5x random (and ≥ 20%): fails** (1.5x at 4 weeks, 2.2x at 8 weeks).
   - The oracle's lead comes from attraction: hidden taste, desirability and warmth. By the world's design (Joel et al. 2017) these cannot be predicted from stated data.
   - On compatibility, slopPack's dates already match the oracle's (section 7).
2. **Adversary contacts and harms cut by ≥ 90%: fails.** The cuts are 39% / 32% (4 weeks) and 25% / 43% (8 weeks).
   - Most adversaries show no signal before their first contact: 3% are not single, and harassers are another group.
   - With PRD 40.5 verification modelled, the cuts rise to 59% / 68%. That is still short, because verification cannot see intent or relationship status.
3. **Back-outs < 10%: fails narrowly** (11.0% / 10.6%). The oracle itself sits at 9.5% / 10.4%.
4. **Lowest group ≥ 0.7x the overall rate: borderline.** Every group at 4 weeks reaches 0.70x. At 8 weeks gay men reach 0.67x (0.73x among members who have any eligible partner).
   - The oracle fails this gate too (0.65x / 0.47x): small pools within a mutual radius run out of partners.

What would move the remaining gates is in section 8.

## 1. Design

Import path: `import { slopPack, makeSlopPack, SLOP_ENGINE_CONFIG, planFirstDate, planFromInput, slopProfiles, distanceBand } from "@thenetwork/engine"`. The source is `packages/engine/src/packs/slop/`.

Every option is in `options.ts`. `makeSlopPack(partialOptions)` builds a variant for ablations.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `zips.ts` | Zip centroids for the three metros (the same table as the world; a test keeps them equal), 0.01° coarse cells, cell distances, distance bands |
| `profile.ts` | A typed dating profile per member, read from facet tags (any scope: the agent may FILTER on agent_private facets but never shows them), plus history from the Network's own records (probe answers, dates, no-shows, back-outs, feedback, blocks, asks) |
| `geo.ts` | `kind: "radius"` GeoModel: a mutual radius on coarse cells, multi-city, travel, distance bands |
| `rules.ts` | Member and pair hard filters (below) |
| `score.ts` | Directional compatibility, the revealed / learned component, logistics, the reciprocal aggregate |
| `generators.ts` | `slop_reciprocal`: reciprocal candidates within geo and filters, top-K per member |
| `assign.ts` | `selection.assign` (greedy or stable roommates, scarce pools first, one thread per member) and `selection.adjust` |
| `plan.ts` | The booked first date: activity, a public venue type, 2-3 time options, a distance band |
| `copy.ts` | Probe text, ask questions, age and distance bands |
| `index.ts` | `makeSlopPack`, `slopPack`, `SLOP_ENGINE_CONFIG` |

**Core changes.** All are additive. The networkPack goldens are byte-identical in both the fast and the full tier.
- Core `City` gains `"la"`, and `NetworkCity` names the Network's two markets. `EngineConfig.timezones` is now `Partial`; `DEFAULT_CONFIG` is unchanged, so every config hash is unchanged.
- `SelectionPolicy.assign`: an optional pack global assignment that replaces the core greedy. The core minors guard still runs on its output.
- `ExplainPack.publicWords`: generic lane words that the leak gate must not treat as private. Slop's facet templates contain "first date idea: coffee" and "dates in their city", so without this every probe that said "date" failed LeakGuard.
- `AskReason` accepts pack reasons (a type-only change).

### 1.1 Geo (radius / zip / multi-city)

- **Location.**
  - A member's zip centroid snaps to a 0.01° cell (about 1 km). The pack computes only on cells and never reads an address or GPS.
  - A multi-city member, or a traveller during a trip they announced, is placed at the market's anchor cell. This is the world's convention ("visitors meet downtown").
- **Mutual radius.** A pair can meet in a market only if:
  - both members date in it this week (a multi-city scope, else the trip market while travelling, else home);
  - and `d(cells) + margin ≤ min(rA, rB)`. The limit is a member's radius, else their stated max miles.
  - The 0.9 mi margin is the worst-case cell error, so a cell distance never admits a pair that is outside a stated radius by centroid distance.
- **Symmetry.** `geo.pairReason` is symmetric; conformance check 8 tests it.
- **Time overlap.** The core city-bucket model, unchanged: home, routine (multi-city) and dated trips.
- **What members see.** Only a band: "under 2 mi", "2-5 mi", "5-10 mi", "10-25 mi" or "25+ mi", in the explanation and the plan. The probe has no area or zip, because slop's probe text ignores the area the core passes.

### 1.2 Hard filters

These run after the core prefixes (minors, holds, paused, blocks) and only ever remove candidates.

| Rule | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Lane | Dating only, one-to-one, no `via` |
| `romance_opt_out` | Opted in to dating (adults only: core) |
| `review_hold` | Held for human review on any of: a `safety:*` cue (scam_pattern, age_signal, photo_mismatch, hostile_language, relationship_signal); a failed verification check (`verify:*:fail`); with `verification.required`, an unverified member; two or more members who blocked them; two or more no-shows with more no-shows than dates. A held member is never matched and never asked |
| `needs_answer` | Unknown orientation, age range or distance: **ask, never guess**. Re-asked weekly. The guess after a silent week is an option, off by default (section 5) |
| `your_turn` | Nothing new while a mutual yes or a booked date waits on the member (`input.openOpportunities`) |
| `recently_matched` | Receptivity pacing: no new probe for 10 days after a date the member liked |
| `already_introduced` | Never re-introduce a revealed pair (booked, backed out, met). A probe that ended before the reveal (an anonymous "no", or silence) may be retried after 7 days, because an anonymous no is about the week, not the person |
| `orientation_mismatch` | Mutual inclusion: each person's matching gender is in the other's seeking set (works for non-bipartite pools) |
| `age_range` | Both ways, on claimed ages |
| `dealbreaker` | Stated dealbreakers both ways, on the other person's known values. An unknown value never counts as a hit |
| Geo `pairReason` | The mutual radius, checked last |

**No race filter.** There is no race or ethnicity field. Tags that look like one (race, ethnicity, health, income...) are dropped when the profile is parsed, and a conformance test checks that adding them changes nothing.

### 1.3 Retrieval and scoring

**Retrieval.** Pairs that share a dating market are checked against the full pair filter. Each member keeps their top 40 by reciprocal value, and a pair is a candidate when it is in either member's top 40. Stated preferences only filter.

**Directional value.** v_a(b) is a's estimated enjoyment of a first date with b, in (0, 1]. It multiplies these factors:
- **Goals:** casual against long-term ×0.6; one unsure ×0.85; unknown ×0.85.
- **Lifestyle clashes:**

  | Clash | Factor |
  |---|---|
  | Smoking: never vs regular | ×0.8 |
  | Drinking: never vs regular | ×0.85 |
  | Faith importance two or more apart | ×0.85 |
  | Politics: left vs right | ×0.75 |
  | Wants kids: yes vs no (not casual) | ×0.7 |
  | Any field unknown on either side | ×0.97 |

- **Likely unstated dealbreakers:** used only when a has not stated any. The other person's values that commonly are someone's dealbreaker, given a's own values.
- **Shared interests:** none ×0.88, one ×0.95, two or more ×1.
- **Activity:** the planned activity is not one a stated, ×0.92.
- **Stated type:** a's stated type against b's self-description, `exp(0.1 · wants·self)`. This is weak by design (Eastwick & Finkel 2008).
- **Learned / revealed component** (`exp(...)` of three terms, each shrunk to a prior with no history):
  - how positively a rates the dates they go on (the actor effect);
  - how the people b met rated b (the partner effect);
  - a's **revealed taste**: is b like the people a rated well? This is a rating-weighted cosine with the self-descriptions of the people a met.
- **Logistics:** a slot both stated as usually free ×1; none ×0.75; unknown ×0.9.

**Reciprocal value.** The harmonic mean (or min) of v_a and v_b. The engine weights make NetValue = mutualBenefit × confidence. There is no warm path, novelty or load term, because dating has no warm graph.

### 1.4 Selection: congestion, fairness, pacing

`selection.assign` replaces the core greedy for this pack.
- **One proposal per member per tick:** one first-date thread at a time, so nobody is flooded.
- **Scarce pools first:** pairs whose scarcer member has at most 6 eligible partners are assigned before the rest.
- **Order within a tier:** the adjusted value, which is `selection.adjust`: an exposure-debt lift (debt carried between runs, capped), plus optional scarcity and popularity terms (off).
- **Assignment:**
  - **Greedy** (the default): a 1/2-approximation of the maximum-weight matching.
  - **Stable roommates** (Irving 1985; unit-tested on a bipartite instance and on a queer pool with an odd preference cycle). Each member ranks partners by their own predicted enjoyment.
    - Phase 1 is proposals, phase 2 is rotation elimination.
    - Members it cannot settle, including when no stable matching exists, are filled greedily.
- **Backup round:** `perMemberPerTick: 2` adds a second proposal the dispatcher sends only if the first fails. It is off by default (section 6).

### 1.5 Consent and the booked first date

- **Probe first** (founder decision): `{ kind: "probe_first", order: "wanter_first", reveal: "opt_out", anonymousProbe: true }`.
  - The member with the live want (asked most recently, then the more responsive one) is probed first, then the partner on a yes.
  - The reveal is the booked plan with an easy opt-out.
  - The probe says it is a date, gives the activity and time options and at most one shareable fact, and never names anyone.
- **The plan** (`planFirstDate`):
  - an activity both stated (short and public first: coffee, drinks, walk);
  - a **public venue type only** (cafe, bar, park, museum...);
  - 60-120 minutes;
  - 2-3 slot options, ranked by P(both free) from stated usual slots and the slots each member attended dates at;
  - the market and a distance band.
- **Attention:** romance ships alone, never in a digest (`shipsAloneLane: "romance"`, `romanceInDigest: false`).

### 1.6 Asks

`selection.extraAsks` emits these through the engine's `planAsks`:
- **Hard fields:** `slop_orientation`, `slop_age_range` and `slop_distance`, all in one message. The member is held until they answer, and re-asked weekly.
- **Compatibility:**
  - `slop_basics`: goal, dealbreakers and lifestyle.
  - `slop_type`: their type and self-description.
  - These do not hold the member back, and are re-asked at most every 3 weeks.

Held members and minors are never asked. In the world, a member answers with p = their reply probability, and the answer shows up in the next week's snapshot.

## 2. Simulation

**The world.** It is unchanged (`packages/worlds/src/slop`): 300 personas per city in SF, NYC and LA, with weekly rounds of the probe-first harness. The baselines reproduce the world doc to the digit (random 8.2%, greedy 7.1%, oracle 36.6% over seeds 1-8).

**Harness additions.** All are optional, so the existing matchers and their numbers are unchanged.
- **Asks:** a matcher may return `{ proposals, asks }`. Answers become visible from the next snapshot, through `SlopNetworkState.learned`, and `snapshot.asks` carries the ask records.
- **Async matchers:** `runSlopWorldAsync`.
- **Verification:** an off-by-default `VerificationModel` (PRD 40.5: liveness and age assurance before the first intro). It writes `verify:liveness|age:pass|fail` facts. The rates are assumptions:
  - liveness fails for 90% of catfish and 70% of romance scammers;
  - age assurance fails for 85% of age liars;
  - honest members fail at 0.5-1%;
  - harassers and members who are not single pass.

**The adapter.** `enginePack.ts`:
- builds the `EngineInput` from the snapshot and `recentAsks`;
- carries `exposureDebt` from one week to the next;
- maps each `EngineProposal` to the harness proposal, with the pack's plan supplying the activity, time options, market and shareable fact;
- maps each `EngineAsk` to a harness ask.

**Evaluation.** `packEval.ts` adds four metrics:
- the **soft second-date rate**: the oracle's pSecond over the dates that happened, from 32 chemistry draws. It is used for tuning, because realized rates are very noisy (below);
- **scammer median reach** (PRD 40.8);
- **fairness among feasible members**: members with at least one stated-mutual partner;
- the **asks** sent and answered.

**Protocol.** Tuning used seeds 1-4 only. The results are from held-out seeds 5-8. Both 4-week and 8-week runs use 300 personas per city.

> **Noise.** The harness keys every random draw by the proposal's index in the week's list, so any change to the proposal list re-draws every outcome. With 4 seeds, the realized second-date rate has an SE of 1-2.5 points, and two variants within about 3 points are a tie. The soft rate (SE 0.5-1) is the steadier comparison.

## 3. Launch gates for "matches go well"

The gates are proposed here, with their justification and the held-out verdicts. Values are the slopPack defaults on seeds 5-8; "+V" is the same pack in a world with the PRD 40.5 verification step modelled.

**Why these gates.**
- **Minors:** declared minors are a policy invariant.
- **Stated-filter violations** are added: proposing outside someone's stated filters is a consent failure, and in this world it ends in a back-out 90% of the time.
- **Safety:** the 90% cuts are the founder's bar, and scammer reach comes from PRD 40.8.
- **Second dates:** the second-date rate is the outcome "matches go well" means. 2.5x random (about 20%) sits between random (8.2%) and the oracle (36.6%).
  - **Second dates per seed** is added so that the rate cannot be won by proposing fewer dates.
  - **Dates per member-month ≥ 0.9x random** is added so that nobody pays for quality with fewer dates.
- **Congestion:** the top-10% share comes from the world doc's congestion failure (greedy 41.5%).
- **Fairness:** the gender and orientation floor makes sure small queer pools are served. A **feasible-member** variant is added, because a member whose stated filters admit nobody cannot be served by any matcher.
- **Back-outs:** a back-out at the reveal is a let-down for the other person.

**Which gates are too strict for this world.**
- **Back-outs < 10%:** the oracle-optimal matcher fails it at 8 weeks.
- **Group floor:** the oracle fails it at both horizons.
- **The 90% safety cuts:** need detection the world gives no matcher before the first contact (section 7).

The recommended replacements are in section 8. The verdicts above are against the gates as proposed.

### What the trail says

**What was adopted, and why.**
- **The compatibility model** is the largest single gain: soft 7.5% to 10.3%. Removing it (section 6) drops the rate back to random.
- **Asking before guessing** (t3) removes every stated-filter violation, and with them most back-outs (19% to 11% at 8 weeks).
- **The basics question** (t4) is the second-largest gain in pair quality (soft +3 to +4 points). Asking for the type (t5) adds little on top.
- **Safety holds** (t2) cut harms by about 40% for a small cost in dates.
- **Re-probing pre-reveal pairs** (t7) and **scarce-first** (t8) were adopted for coverage and fairness: second dates per seed rise 25.5 to 31.2, and the lowest group (8 weeks) rises 0.43 to 0.59.
  - Without re-probing, small pools run dry. On an earlier build, turning it off cut second dates per seed from 35.5 to 25.0 at 8 weeks, and the lowest group from 0.61 to 0.26.

**How the defaults were chosen.** Earlier tuning rounds, on the same seeds but with the guess-after-silence default, chose greedy over stable, harmonic over min, scarce degree 6 over 4 and type weight 0.1 over 0.3. These are the rows marked x.
- In those rounds greedy and stable tied on soft rate, and stable took about 3x the run time.
- An offline proposal-level check (the oracle pSecond of week-0 proposals) favoured type weight 0.1 (20.9%) over 0.3 (19.8%) when every member's type is known.
- On the final rerun above, stable roommates, type weight 0.3 and scarce degree 4 look as good or better on the tuning seeds. The differences are within noise. They were not adopted after the held-out run, to avoid tuning on held-out seeds.
- **Scarce degree 4** (fairness 0.79-0.89 at 8 weeks on the tuning seeds) is the first candidate for the next round, with more seeds.

**Guess after silence.** After the first held-out run, guessing after a silent week (claimed age ± 0, within 5 mi) produced 1-3 stated-filter violations per seed: some members' stated range excludes their own age.
- The default became the founder's rule (ask, never guess), and the held-out evaluation was rerun once.
- That was a policy correction, not a performance tweak. The guess remains an option.

## 7. Why the safety and second-date gates fail

### 7.1 Second-date rate

**Where the oracle's lead comes from.** A diagnostic on an earlier build (tuning seeds 1-4, 4 weeks) compared the dates each matcher produced:
- **Compatibility (min of both sides):**
  - slopPack 0.74;
  - the oracle 0.67;
  - random 0.50.
- **Pre-date attraction:** slopPack 0.14, against the oracle's 0.30.

In the world, attraction is `warmth + 0.9·desirability + 1.8·taste·traits − 0.4·own desirability`: pair-specific taste with an SD of 1.8 on the logit, plus the actor and partner effects. Only the stated type (correlation about 0.35 with revealed taste, and only once asked) and a few post-date ratings carry any of it.

**The information ceiling.** The oracle pSecond of week-0 proposals (honest pairs) rises as the member answers questions:

| What the agent knows | pSecond |
|---|---:|
| Nothing asked yet | 14.5% |
| Basics answered | 18.7% |
| Basics and type answered | 20.9% |

So with all stated information, a visible-data matcher sits near 20% before adversaries, and the realized rate is lower again because 13% of proposals involve an adversary who escaped detection. The 2.5x gate needs either:
- many more dates per member, so that revealed taste can be learned; the trend is already visible from 4 to 8 weeks (12.7% to 15.6%);
- or signals the world does not give: photos, conversation, the probe answer depending on the person.

### 7.2 Safety

Held-out contacts by adversary kind, 4 weeks (per seed, counted per side):

| | romance scammer | catfish | harasser | not single | age liar |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| random | 10.5 | 15.5 | 15.8 | 18.3 | 5.3 |
| slopPack | 6.0 | 11.5 | 9.0 | 11.3 | 3.0 |
| slopPack + verification | 2.5 | 1.5 | 8.5 | 11.8 | 0.5 |

Harms per seed, 4 weeks:

| | money ask | off-platform move | financial loss | harassment | catfish reveal | deception | minor contact |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| random | 5.5 | 10.0 | 0.8 | 8.5 | 4.0 | 2.3 | 5.3 |
| slopPack | 4.5 | 5.3 | 0.5 | 4.3 | 4.0 | 3.3 | 3.0 |
| slopPack + verification | 1.8 | 2.5 | 0.5 | 4.8 | 0.5 | 1.3 | 0.5 |

**What the world gives before a first contact.**
- **Safety cues:** the agent notices a cue in chat 15-50% of the time (5-25% for "not single"), with a 1% false-positive rate per cue on honest members.
- **Behaviour:** adversaries say yes to almost every probe. The probe is anonymous, though, and an "eager yes" trust rule (3 or 4 yeses, no noes) did not separate them from eager honest members (tested on seeds 1-4: no gain).
- **After a contact:** reports put the offender on a hold, which keeps scammer reach at 0.5-0.75. The harm of that first contact still happens.

**Members who are not single (3%) and harassers (1.5%)** account for about 80% of the adversary contacts left after verification, and neither shows up before a contact.

**What a 90% cut would need.** Product mechanisms the PRD lists that the world does not model:
- the relay with a scam classifier, which blocks money asks and off-platform moves at the reveal;
- harassment detection in the relay;
- photos in the probe;
- a relationship-status attestation.

## 8. Remaining gaps and recommendations

1. **Gates to revise (proposal):**
   - **Back-outs:** "≤ 0.75x random" (slopPack 0.61-0.63x: pass) rather than < 10%.
   - **Fairness:** the floor for groups among feasible members (pass at both horizons), plus a separate report of members with no feasible partner. This is a supply problem for growth, not one for the matcher.
   - **Safety:** split into "dangerous adversaries (scammer, catfish, age liar) cut ≥ 90% with verification" and harms ≥ 90% with the relay scam classifier. Both need product features (PRD 40.5) before they can be met.
   - **Second dates:** keep the 2.5x target as a north star; measure it over 8+ weeks.
2. **Model the relay and photos in the world:** the scam classifier on relay messages, harassment reports in the relay, and photos in the probe. These are the levers left for safety and back-outs.
3. **Ask volume:** about 1,600-1,700 asks per seed (most are the one-time basics and type questions at week 0). The hard-field re-ask is weekly with no cap; the platform should cap it at 2 and then wait for the member.
4. **Coverage:** 8.5-10% of members get no proposal. Review holds with no human-review loop are the main cause; in production a reviewer clears false positives (about 5% of honest members).
5. **More seeds:** 4 tuning seeds cannot separate variants within about 2 points. Next: tune on 8 or more seeds and pair harness draws by pair rather than proposal index (a world change), then decide stable vs greedy, scarce degree 4 vs 6 and type weight.
6. **Not built:**
   - a dating judge rubric (`judge` is absent, so the LLM passes never run for slop);
   - double-date or group formats;
   - long distance;
   - per-member time zones inside multi-city;
   - a fitted attention calibrator (the knots are placeholders);
   - zips beyond the 51 in the table.
7. **Attraction:** the stated type is weak by design, so revealed taste from post-date ratings is the right channel. The world's 4-8 weeks give each member only 0-4 ratings.

## 9. What the platform session needs to wire `slop:nyc`

**Engine call (per tick, nightly or on demand).**

```ts
runEngine(input, { ...SLOP_ENGINE_CONFIG, cities: ["nyc"], seed }, { pack: makeSlopPack({ verification: { required: true } }) })
```

**Snapshot fields** (`EngineInput`). Everything else is the core snapshot; `packages/worlds/src/slop/enginePack.ts slopEngineInput` is the reference mapping.

| Field | What to send |
|---|---|
| `Member` | Claimed and verified age; `homeCity: "nyc"`; `state`; `prefs.romanceOptIn` and `categoriesOptIn: ["romance"]` (adults only) |
| Facets | The tag schema in `packs/slop/profile.ts`. `romance:is/seeks/age:`, `slop:zip:`, `slop:scope:` and `slop:max_miles:`, `slop:goal:`, `slop:<value>:`, `slop:dealbreaker:`, `slop:activity:`, `slop:free:`, `slop:wants:` and `slop:self:` are agent_private or matchable, as in the world mapping. Interests: shareable when the member allows. Safety cues from the agent: `safety:*` |
| Verification facts | `verify:liveness:pass\|fail` and `verify:age:pass\|fail` (PRD 40.5) |
| Presence | home with `areas: ["zip:<zip>"]`; routine for multi-city; temporary for trips (from / to) |
| Intents | One romance intent per member; `createdAt` is the last inbound "find me a date" (the live want, so they are probed first) |
| `interactions` | One per proposal: outcome, `acceptedBy` / `declinedBy` / `noResponse`; `at` = the date's time for completed and no_show |
| `feedback` | Sentiment and `wouldMeetAgain` |
| `edges` | `met`, `blocked` |
| `safetyHolds` | From reports |
| `recentAsks` | `{ memberId, reason, at, answeredAt }` |
| `exposureDebt` | The previous run's `runLog.exposureDebt` |
| `openOpportunities` | A mutual yes waiting, a booked date ahead: the "your turn" limit |

**Ask handling.**
- `result.asks` carries reasons `slop_orientation`, `slop_age_range`, `slop_distance`, `slop_basics` and `slop_type`, with question text.
- Send them as profiling questions: they do not count against the cap (`notInvites`). Hard-field asks for one member go in one message.
- Record an `AskRecord`, and set `answeredAt` when the member replies.
- Parse the answer into the facet tags above. The member stays held (`needs_*`) until then; cap re-asks at 2.

**Probes.**
- `attention.buildProbe(w, spec, recipient, [other], now)`, with `spec.options` set to the plan's time options. The text is `slopProbeText`.
- PRD 40.5 also wants an age band and a distance band in the probe. Add them per recipient with the exported `ageBand` and `distanceBand` (from the plan) before the leak gate.
- Sequential: the first member (`participants[0]`) first, then the partner on a yes.
- Romance always ships alone. Expiry 24 hours.

**Plan booking** (after the mutual yes).
1. `planFromInput(input, first, partner, ["nyc"], pack.options)` returns the market, activity, public venue type, minutes, options (slot ids into `SLOTS`) and a distance band.
2. The platform resolves:
   - the slots to concrete times in the market's time zone;
   - a concrete **public** venue of that type near the midpoint of the two cells (a venue DB, never a home);
   - books the slot both picked.
3. It sends the reveal as the booked plan ("You're both in: coffee, Thu 7pm near …; reply if you can't make it").
4. It turns on share-my-date and schedules the check-in text after the date.

**Then record:**
- the interaction outcome;
- feedback (it feeds the learned component);
- a `met` edge for a date that happened;
- `blocked` plus a safety hold for reports.
