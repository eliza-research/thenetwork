## Result in brief (iteration 1)

**The pack passes every safety, fairness and efficiency gate, but it does not produce more hires.** On the held-out seeds 5-8 (400 candidates and 80 jobs per city, 8 weeks):

| | Job board (keyword) | Oracle upper bound | peonPack |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hires per seed | 47.8 ± 2.6 | 52.0 ± 3.7 | 45.3 ± 1.9 |
| Intros sent | 16,729 | 3,240 | 1,555 |
| Qualified share of intros | 12% | 33% | 75% |
| Interviews per hire | 6.5 | 3.4 | 4.3 |
| Applications expired unread | 1,707 | 231 | 180 |
| Scam reach per seed | 25.5 | 0 | 1.0 |
| Unverified-employer intros | 1,692 | 0 | 0 |
| Intros to jobs with no pay range | 1,135 | 141 | 0 |
| Fake candidates reaching employers | 122 | 0 | 10 |
| Minors matched | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Gate outcomes.**

- **Pass:**
  - minors matched = 0;
  - impact ratio ≥ 0.8 for every sealed group at the automated stage (0.96 pooled; 0.86 in the worst seed);
  - interviews per hire ≤ 12;
  - 90-day retention ≥ keyword on the held-out seeds (98.2% vs 96.3%).
- **Fail:**
  - **Hires ≥ 2x keyword.** Measured 0.95x.
  - **Under-applied roles filled ≥ 1.5x keyword.** Measured 0.78x.
  - **Scam reach ≤ 1 in every seed.** The worst held-out seed had 2.
  - **Retention ≥ keyword on the tuning seeds.** Missed by 0.5 points, which is within noise.

**Why the two hires gates cannot pass.** They are infeasible in this world:

- The **oracle**, which has hidden truth, reaches only 1.09x keyword on hires and 0.94x on under-applied hires.
- In a pilot market of 400 candidates and 80 jobs per city, hires are limited by how many qualified candidate-job pairs exist, not by how they are found.
- A 4x thicker market (1,600 candidates per city) shows the same pattern: keyword 79.3, oracle 86.0, pack 77.5.
- Horton (2017) found **+20%** fill from algorithmic recommendations in a much larger market. That is the empirically supported size of the effect, not 2x.

Section 6 proposes replacement gates, with the reasoning.

**Where the pack's value is.**

- It makes the same number of hires with **one tenth of the intros**.
- 75% of its intros are qualified, against 12% for keyword.
- It needs about **a third fewer interviews per hire**.
- It sends **no** intros to unverified employers or to jobs without a pay range.
- Scam reach is about 1 per seed, against 25.
- Concentration is lower: the top 10% of jobs get 31% of applications, against 41%.
- It shows **no adverse impact at the stage it controls**.

The four-fifths check works: an ablation that screens by graduation year drops the 40+ ratio to **0.68**, and the check catches it.

### 1.1 Population (per city: NYC and SF)

**Role families.** The seven families are software engineer (15-1252), data analyst (15-2051), accountant (13-2011), registered nurse (29-1141), customer support (43-4051), sales rep (41-4012) and warehouse associate (53-7062).

- Candidate shares are tilted toward tech: software engineer is 24% of candidates but 14% of jobs.
- Job shares are tilted toward care, logistics, accounting and sales: nurse is 6% of candidates but 14% of jobs, warehouse 8% vs 15%.
- So some roles are swamped and others under-applied, by construction (domain research B2).

**Candidates: 400 per city.** 60% are present at week 0; the rest join in weeks 1-6.

| Part | Hidden truth | Stated (what the agent learns) |
|---|---|---|
| Skills | True level 0-5 per skill, tracking seniority; 1-2 adjacent-family skills | Claimed levels with resume inflation: -1 (8%), 0 (50%), +1 (27%), +2 (11%), +3 (4%). Keyword stuffing: 30% chance of claiming a family skill at level 2-3 when the true level is ≤ 1. 25% of skills have demonstrated evidence (portfolio or reference), which is honest. |
| Role family, seniority | True family; seniority from true age (1 + (age - 22)/11 + noise, so it is correlated with a sealed attribute but job-related) | Target families (30% add an adjacent one); seniority (12% one level high) |
| Pay | True floor ≈ 0.85 x market median | Stated floor ≈ true x 1.02 (an expectation, never salary history) |
| Location | Home area and zip; acceptable work models; commute areas | The same work models and commute areas (candidate-set); the zip only as an agent_private proxy |
| Logistics | - | Authorized to work (97%); needs sponsorship (12% in tech, 3% otherwise); licences (RN, CDL, CPA); start in 0-4 weeks; current employer excluded (15%) |
| Behaviour | Weekly search activity U(0.45, 1); reply probability about N(0.85, 0.1); interview noise; 90-day retention propensity; a hidden fit per candidate-job pair | - |

**Adversaries and minors (candidates).**

- **Fake candidates (3%).** True skills are ≤ 1 and claims are +3. The agent notices a cue (identity or resume inconsistency) in 50% of them, and falsely in 0.5% of real candidates.
- **Minors aged 13-17 (3%).** They declare their age; nobody lies about age here. Minors may join but are never matched.

**Companies and job seats: 40 companies and 80 jobs per city.** 50% of jobs are posted at week 0 and the rest in weeks 1-5.

- **Job fields:** role family, seniority 1-5, title, pay range, mode, site area, 1-3 must-haves (minimum level about 0.6 x seniority + 0.7), 1-2 nice-to-haves, openings (1: 65%, 2: 25%, 3: 10%), urgency, visa sponsorship and a required licence.
- **Pay ranges:** 8% of honest postings arrive without one.
- **Remote:** 0-45% of jobs per family are remote.
- **Hidden per job:** an offer-bar shift, a brand appeal, a weekly review capacity of 4-8, and whether the job is real.

**Adversary companies.**

- **Scam (5%).** About 75% fail verification. Those that pass impersonate a real brand, and 60% of them show a scam cue at intake. Their jobs are remote, about 35% above market pay, and have a single low must-have.
- **Discriminatory (8%).** The target is one of 40+, women, race group b, race group c or disability. They reject the target group with p = 0.85 whenever they can see it: at review when they see a full resume, and always at the interview. 60% asked at intake for a protected-trait filter, which the agent refused and logged.
- **Ghosting (10%).** Responsiveness is 0.1-0.3 a week.

Seeds 1-4 happen to contain no scam that passed verification without a cue. Seeds 5, 6 and 7 each contain one. So the scam gate is genuinely tested only on held-out data.

**The sealed store** (synthetic voluntary self-ID) holds:

- sex: f 48%, m 48%, x 4%;
- race / ethnicity group: a 45%, b 22%, c 20%, d 13%;
- age: about N(36, 10.5), 18-67;
- disability: 9%;
- caregiver: 32% of women, 17% of men.

**Only the audit and the metrics read it.**

**Proxies correlate with the sealed attributes, as the domain research asks (B5):**

| Proxy | Correlated with | How |
|---|---|---|
| Home area and zip | Race / ethnicity | Area weights by group follow residential segregation. In NYC, group b lives in the Bronx at 30% and group a at 3%. Within an area, the zip is also skewed by group. |
| Graduation year | Age | Birth year + 22-24 |
| Employment gaps | Caregiver status, disability (and through caregiving, sex) | 45% of caregivers or disabled candidates have a gap, against 12% of others |
| First and last names | Sex and race / ethnicity | Drawn from group-specific pools |

### 1.3 Snapshot (`snapshot.ts`)

`buildPeonSnapshot(pop, state)` returns an `EngineInput` in the tag schema of `packs/peon/schema.ts`.

**Member records.**

- **Candidates** are members with `peon:entity:candidate`.
- **A job is a seat:** one member per open job, held by the verified hiring manager.
  - `Member.name` is the manager's name; `Member.state` is `"open"`.
  - The seat has a job intent "Hire: <title>" (details `peon:job`).
  - Capacity is in `peon:openings:<n>`, decremented as hires land; the intent closes at 0.
- **Ages.** Adults carry `Member.age = 18`, which encodes an 18+ confirmation and never the real age. Declared minors carry their stated age, which trips the core minors gate.

**Facets.** Hiring data rides on tags, so no core type changed.

| Data | Tags | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Skills | `peon:skill:<skill>:<claimed level>`; demonstrated evidence as provenance `connected_source` | The 3 strongest claims shareable (the candidate-approved summary); the rest matchable |
| Interview feedback | `peon:demonstrated:<skill>:<level>`, written after interviews with p = 0.7 | matchable |
| Pay floor, work models, commute areas, authorization, sponsorship, start date, licences | (one tag each) | matchable |
| Excluded company | `peon:exclude:<company>` boundary | agent_private |
| Company, title, pay range, location, must-haves, nice-to-haves | `peon:company:*`, `peon:pay:<min>-<max>`, `peon:must:<skill>:<min>` | shareable |
| Verified flag, headcount, urgency, sponsorship | (one tag each) | matchable |
| Scam cue, discriminatory request | `safety:scam_pattern`, `safety:discriminatory_request` | agent_private, inferred |
| Proxies | `peon:proxy:zip:*`, `peon:proxy:grad_year:*`, `peon:proxy:gap_months:*` | agent_private |
| Canary | (one per candidate) | agent_private |

**History.** Interactions, one per intro, carry outcome, acceptedBy, declinedBy and noResponse. The snapshot also carries `recentProposals` shells, safety holds and block edges.

**Tests.** Changing every sealed attribute and every hidden field leaves the snapshot byte-identical. No sealed attribute, true age, adversary label or hidden field name appears in it.

### 1.4 Oracle (`oracle.ts`, `ORACLE_PARAMS`)

All draws are keyed by (seed, member, job, week), so every arm sees the same draw for the same event.

- **P(candidate interested)** = σ(-0.6 + 2.2·payHead + 0.9·family + 0.7·mode + 0.45·appeal + seniority step - 1.0·[no range]).
  - payHead is the pay over the TRUE floor.
  - The result is x 0.15 if the candidate's true logistics rule the job out.
  - Fake candidates say yes 90% of the time.
- **P(employer acceptable).** Computed from what the employer sees:
  - Formula: σ(-1.0 + 4.0·(claimed must-have coverage - 0.6) + 0.8·(1 - |stated seniority - job seniority|)).
  - In the job-board arms the employer sees a full resume, so discriminatory employers reject their target group here.
- **Interview.** After an employer yes, the candidate shows up with p = 0.92 x max(0.6, reply probability).
- **P(pass).** σ((quality - 0.5 - bar shift) / 0.33).
  - Quality = the TRUE skill margin over the must-haves + nice-to-haves - 0.45·|seniority gap| + a hidden pair fit (N(0, 0.45)).
  - It is 0 if the employer cannot legally hire the candidate (licence, authorization, sponsorship).
  - Discriminatory employers fail their target group with p = 0.85.
  - The passers compete for the openings by interview score.
- **P(accept).** σ(-0.5 + 3.0·payHead + 0.5·mode + 0.3·appeal + 0.5·family). The candidate takes the best of the week's offers.
- **P(90-day retention).** σ(2.0 + 1.1·quality + 1.5·payHead + 0.6·mode + propensity).
- **Quality of hire** = σ(quality) when retained, 0 otherwise.
- **Qualified pair** (a metric): the true logistics fit, the candidate truly meets the must-haves (at most one level short on one), seniority is within ±1, and the true floor is ≤ the top of the range.

### 1.5 Harness (`world.ts`): one week

1. **Snapshot**, then **matcher** (it reads only the snapshot).
2. **Platform rules for every arm:** declared minors and held members are never delivered. A proposal with a minor counts as a violation. Each pair is introduced once.
3. **Weekly cap on probes per candidate:** 3 for the pack and the oracle, 10 for the job board.
4. **Candidate-first probe.** The candidate sees the role, pay, mode and company, and replies (active that week x reply probability). A yes is an application.
5. **Offers** for the interviews held the week before.
6. **Interviews** scheduled for this week, with feedback recorded.
7. **Employer review.**
   - If the employer is responsive this week, it reads up to its review capacity in arrival order.
   - Acceptable applications are **ranked by how they look on paper**, and the best fill next week's interview slots (1 + open headcount).
   - Acceptable applications without a slot stay on file. Any application expires after 3 weeks.
   - **A scam employer accepts everyone.** The contact either:
     - is caught by peon's relay scam screen (p = 0.7, pack only, an assumed recall), which holds the company; or
     - reaches the candidate (scam reach), who reports with p = 0.6, which holds the company and blocks the seat.
8. **Exits:** each week 2% of searching candidates leave for reasons outside the platform.
9. **Audit** in weeks 3 and 7 (pack only). Flagged companies go to human review; the `audit-hold` ablation suspends them instead.

### 3.1 Jobs as seats (the entity choice)

The core `Member` model has no job entity. A **job is a seat**: one member per open job, held by its hiring manager, with an intent "Hire: <title>" and capacity `peon:openings:<n>`.

**Why seats, and not jobs as extra intents of one employer member.** The engine:

- dedupes candidates by participant set, so one employer member would get at most one job per candidate per run;
- budgets per member;
- reads `timesSelected(member)` in `selection.adjust`.

One member per job gives per-job dedupe, per-job capacity and per-job congestion control with **no engine change**.

**Company-level concerns** run through `peon:company:<id>`: verification, holds, the audit, current-employer exclusions and the new-employer rate limit.

**Seat state.** Seats are in state `"open"` with a large `open` budget. Employers get a weekly slate capped per job (below), not an interruption budget. Candidates are `"normal"`, with 3 roles per 7 days.

**Shortcomings.**

- A hiring manager with three jobs appears as three seats.
- Member-level attention for employers (one digest per person) would need a person-to-seat map at the attention layer. That is not needed for the sim.

### 3.2 Hard filters (`rules.ts`; after the core minors, holds, pause and blocks prefix)

**Member rules.**

- `peon_not_onboarded`
- Job seats: `job_closed`, `employer_unverified`, `no_pay_range`, `employer_scam_signal`
- Candidates: `not_searching`, `identity_signal` (the fake-candidate cue), `interruption_budget`, `active_intro_cap` (5 in flight)

**Pair rules.**

- `not_candidate_job`
- `already_introduced` (any earlier interaction or proposal)
- `excluded_company` (current-employer blocking)
- `seniority_band` (±1, stated)
- `pay_below_floor` (stated floor vs the top of the range)
- `work_authorization` (authorized; sponsorship offered if needed)
- `credential_required`

**Geo (`peonGeo.pairReason`).**

- `location_incompatible`: a remote job needs a candidate who wants remote.
- Onsite and hybrid jobs need the same market, the same mode, and a site area in the candidate's own commute list.
- The home zip is never read.

**Configuration rule.** `intro_shape`: exactly one candidate and one seat, and no `via`.

**Changed in tuning.** Role family is **not** a hard filter. Domain research B3 lists it as soft ("career-intent fit"). As a hard filter it dropped qualified adjacent-family pairs that the job board found.

### 3.3 Retrieval and scoring

**Two-way retrieval.**

- `candidate_to_jobs` gives the top 8 jobs per candidate.
- `job_to_candidates` gives the top 14 candidates per job.
- Both are drawn from one table of hard-filtered pairs whose must-have coverage is at least 0.55.

**Reciprocal value.** The value is the harmonic mean of two sides.

**need (employer side):**

- must-have coverage, using **evidence-weighted** skill levels:
  - an unverified claim is discounted by 0.4 levels;
  - demonstrated levels and interview feedback are taken at face value;
- nice-to-haves;
- seniority fit.

**want (candidate side):**

- pay headroom over the stated floor;
- role-family preference (soft: first family 1.0, second 0.8, other 0.25);
- work-mode preference;
- seniority step.

**Engine scoring (`PEON_ENGINE_CONFIG`).**

- Weights: `fit` 1, `mutualBenefit` 1, `socialRisk` 0.2; the Network's social terms (novelty, warm path, timing, activation, load, repetition) are 0.
- Thresholds: 0.12. Exploration: 0. Complementarity: off.

### 3.4 Congestion control (`selection.ts`, via `selection.adjust`)

The adjusted value is -Infinity past a cap, so the core greedy skips the candidate. Congestion follows ReCon (RecSys 2023) and LinkedIn (RecSys 2017).

**Per job: a weekly slate.**

- The slate target is min(5, 2 + openings) candidate yeses (the 3-5 slate in domain research B4).
- Applications waiting for review count against it.
- Probes = the remaining yeses / 0.45 (the expected yes rate). No pile-ups, no ghosted queues.

**Per candidate:** 3 roles a week.

**Adjustments to the value.**

- A spreading penalty as a slate fills (0.06 x fill).
- A lift for **under-applied** jobs (Horton 2017): a small hard-filtered pool, or few applications per opening (0.08).
- A lift for candidates with no intro yet (0.04).
- Multiplication by the employer's **learned responsiveness**: (answered + 2) / (answered + ghosted + 2.5) from the interaction log.
- **New-employer rate limit:** at most 3 probes a week until the company has answered an application (domain research B2, "per-employer rate limits").

**Exposure floor off.** The core exposure-floor pass is turned off (`exposureFloorShare: 0`) because it takes picks without calling `selection.adjust`, which would bypass the slate caps. This is noted under engine seams (section 11).

### 3.5 Consent, slates and copy

**Consent** is `{ kind: "application", initiator: "seeker", reviewer: "provider", blindReview: [name, age, zip, grad_year, employment_gaps, photo, pronouns, school], stages: [...] }`. `startProbeFlowFor` probes the candidate first (role `seeker`), and the employer only after the candidate's yes. This is tested.

**Employers see a blind summary** (`buildSlate`):

- **unranked**, in seeded random order;
- with must-have checkmarks (yes / partial / no) from the candidate's own claims;
- with "within your pay range" and the start date;
- never a score, a rank or a name.

**Leak gate on copy.**

- Every sentence of an explanation must pass the core leak gate on its own, or it is dropped.
- The fallback text uses only words of 3 letters or fewer, which the leak gate always lets through.
- **Probe text:** "A verified employer is hiring for …".
- **Never-asks line:** "We never ask for money, bank details or your SSN." It is not written as "peon.biz never asks", because the core guard reads a domain as contact details.

### 3.6 The protected-attribute firewall

1. **Sealed attributes never enter a snapshot.** Tested by perturbation: the snapshot stays byte-identical.
2. **`profile.ts` is the only reader** of member data.
   - It reads matchable and shareable facets, plus two kinds of agent_private facet:
     - the excluded-company boundary;
     - the agent's own safety cues.
   - It skips every `peon:proxy:*` tag and never reads `Member.name` or `Member.age`.
3. **Tests (`packages/engine/test/peon-pack.test.ts`):**
   - Changing every proxy (zip, graduation year, gaps), every name and every adult's age leaves the proposals, scores, explanations, ranks and scored log byte-identical.
   - **Mutation check:** adding a single name-length term to fit makes the test fail.
4. **End-to-end test (`packages/worlds/test/peon.test.ts`):**
   - Remove the discriminatory employers.
   - Permute every sealed attribute (sex, race, age, disability).
   - Three weeks of the pack's flows are identical: keys, candidate yeses, employer yeses and interviews.
5. **The judge context** (`peonJudgeContext`) is criteria-only and pseudonymous (P1 / P2). The test checks that it has no zip, graduation year, gap, age or name, and no proxy value.
6. **Discriminatory requests** at intake are agent_private cues. They never become a criterion, and the pack has no mechanism to apply them. The `proxy-leak` ablation shows what happens if something did apply them.

### 3.7 Conformance

**`runConformance(peonPack)` passes (11 checks)** on two kinds of world:

- the pack's testkit worlds (`test/peon-conformance.test.ts`);
- simulated snapshots with 8% minors (`worlds/test/peon.test.ts`).

**peon-specific tests:**

- registration;
- the firewall (proxies, names and ages);
- no minors, unverified or scam-cued employers, jobs without a range, or excluded companies;
- candidate-first consent;
- slate and candidate caps;
- unranked slates.

**Mutation check:** removing the `employer_unverified` rule fails the "never proposes" test.

## 4. Tuning (seeds 1-4 only)

**Changes made while building, before the held-out run:**

- Role family made soft (section 3.2).
- Slate caps counted in candidate yeses (the first version counted probes, which starved candidates; zero-intro share was 33%).
- Core exposure floor off (it bypassed the caps).
- Learned employer responsiveness and the new-employer rate limit (both from domain research B2).
- The audit's p-value bar tightened from 0.05 to 0.005, Bonferroni-like, because false flags were 8.8 per seed.

**Sweep of the pack's knobs** (`tune.ts`, seeds 1-4, mean hires; the SE on hires is about 4):

**How each stage is measured.**

- Each stage's selection rate is the share of a sealed group that reached the stage, out of the stage's pool.

## 10. Limits

- **The world is a model.** Its parameters are calibrated to benchmarks where the small market allows (section 2), not fitted to peon data. Recalibrate when real outcomes exist.
- **Thin market by spec** (400 x 80 per city). The interview economics (interviews per hire, offer rate) do not reach industry benchmarks, and time-to-fill is compressed.
- **Assumed parameters:**
  - the relay scam screen's recall (0.7);
  - the candidate's scam report probability (0.6);
  - the agent's cue rates (scam 60% of verified scams; fake candidates 50%; discriminatory requests 60%).
- **No conversation.** Onboarding is reduced to the stated profile. There are no reschedules, counter-offers or multi-round loops beyond one interview week and one decision week.
- **Not modelled:** employers' review accuracy falling with volume, candidate discouragement after ghosting, and referral (warm-path) hires. Each would probably favour the pack. They were left out rather than added to reach a gate.
- **The 4-seed groups are small.** Differences under about 2 SE (hires SE about 2-5) are noise.

- Both probation rules hold scam reach to **≤ 1 in every tuning seed**. Without a limit, it reaches 2 in two seeds.
- The cost is hires. "Until first interview" costs 3.1 hires per seed against no limit; "until first answer" costs 1.5.
- On the tuning seeds, "until first answer" gives the same scam protection as "until first interview" at a lower cost. **It is the tuning-selected default** (`probationUntil: "answered"`).
- **Disclosure:** the held-out seeds 9-12 had already been run once with the interview rule before this default was set. The choice follows the seeds 1-8 numbers above. Every variant's held-out result is reported in I2.6, so nothing is hidden.

Why the coordinator's rule costs more:

- At cold start every company is new.
- An honest employer's first interview happens at the earliest in week 1, and is visible from week 2.
- So the rule throttles every employer for two weeks, while the deadlines of urgent roles (4 weeks) are running.

### I2.8 What is still missing

- **Hires are not doubled.** The two original volume gates fail on held-out data, as in iteration 1. The difference: the world now shows *where* the pack wins, which are attention, time, scams, concentration and under-applied roles on most seeds.
- **The pack's remaining hire losses are timing losses:**
  - the cold-start new-employer limit;
  - weekly slate pacing against 4-week deadlines on urgent roles.
- **A better fix is to probe in proportion to remaining time rather than in fixed slates.** This is not tested, because tuning more on these seeds would contaminate the next held-out set.
  - The urgency and stale-job lifts tried on seeds 1-8 did not help (39.4 and 39.6 vs 39.8).
- **Recruiter costs are assumptions** (6 minutes to read, 1.5 hours per interview, 4-6 hours a week), and so are the fatigue and trust rates.
- **The relay scam screen's recall (0.7) is assumed.** Without it the new-employer limit carries more of the load.
