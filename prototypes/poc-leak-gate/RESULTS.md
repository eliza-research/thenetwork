# POC: outbound leak gate (P21, PRD risk R6)

**Question.** Can an outbound leak gate (deterministic checks plus an LLM inference classifier) reach at least 99.5% recall on seeded leaks with at most 2% false positives on clean outbound, including paraphrase, inference and timing leaks?

**Answer: almost, but not as an unattended gate.** On 972 held-out seeded leaks and 972 clean messages, the frozen gate (deterministic core plus gpt-6-luna, prompt v2) caught **98.97%** of leaks (95% CI 98.1 to 99.4) with **1.85%** false positives (1.2 to 2.9).

- **Exact leaks pass the bar.** Exact copies, paraphrases, timing leaks, canaries, contact details, addresses, routines and non-participant names were all at 100% recall.
- **Inference leaks miss it.** Every one of the 10 misses was a subtle inference leak.
- **Most misses are bad test data.** An independent model (deepseek-v4-pro, a third model family) judged 6 of the 10 missed leaks not to be leaks at all, and 3 as weak. Counting only leaks it judged clear, recall is **99.87%** (758/759, CI 99.3 to 100).
- **What is not proven.** The ≥99.5% target is not shown for subtle inference leaks. The corpus has only 29 clear inference leaks, so the 99.87% mainly reflects the easier classes.
- **False positives are close to the 2% limit.** The rate stayed between 1.2% and 3.2% across prompts and repeat runs.
- **Model output varies between runs.** A second classifier run on the same data moved recall by 0.3 points and the false-positive rate by 0.3 points.

> **Update: see [Fixes](#fixes-after-review)** below. That section adds contact scrubbing for the LLM, scope-aware handling of the recipient's own facts, a `decide()` pipeline (SEND / HOLD_REVIEW / BLOCK), the switch to prompt v3, a cross-message pattern check, and a fresh test3 split. Every number above is from before those fixes and is kept unchanged.

Everything here is synthetic. The test data was generated by claude-sonnet-4.5, the gate used gpt-6-luna, and the label audit used deepseek-v4-pro (an earlier audit pass used gemini-3.1-pro), all on Surplus.

## What was built

| Piece | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gate contract and visibility model | `src/types.ts`, `src/visibility.ts` | Scopes are `public`, `network` and `agent_private`. They map from the dataset's `shareable`, `matchable` and `agent_private`. The recipient always sees their own facets. Co-participants see each other's public and network facets. `agent_private` is never shown to anyone except its owner. Non-participants are invisible entirely, including their names. |
| Deterministic layer | `src/deterministic.ts` | Reuses `checkMessage` from `@thenetwork/judge` `rules.ts` for the phone, email, address and canary regexes. Adds: normalized canary matching plus a ref-code-shape check; a contact registry match that handles "at/dot" and spaced digits; street-name matching against invisible addresses (public venue addresses are allowed); non-participant names from a 500-member directory; and n-gram and fuzzy overlap against recipient-invisible facets, minus the words the recipient can already see (the `privateVocabulary` idea from `engine/explain.ts`). A separate **lexicon** sub-layer flags sensitive-topic words (divorce, sober, pregnant, laid off, …) that no visible facet supports. |
| LLM classifier | `src/classifier.ts` | `defaultLLM()`, which is Surplus gpt-6-luna. The JSON puts reasoning first, then quote, category and the `leak` verdict. If the output fails to parse three times, the gate holds the message (fails closed). Prompts are versioned v1, v2 and v3. |
| Gate | `src/gate.ts` | Runs the deterministic layer first and skips the LLM if that already holds the message. Returns `pass` with a minted `LeakCheckedMessage`, or `hold` with findings. |
| Brand enforcement | `src/brand.ts`, `tests/bypass.typecheck.ts`, `tests/brand.test.ts` | `send()` only accepts `LeakCheckedMessage`. That type is branded with a private `unique symbol`, and only `gate.ts` imports `mintLeakChecked`; it is not exported from `index.ts`. Five `// @ts-expect-error` bypass attempts must fail to compile, and the test runs `tsc` on them: a raw string, a look-alike object literal, an unbranded variable, importing the minter, and sending an un-narrowed hold. A negative control confirms the bypass really is a compile error. A source scan bans `as LeakCheckedMessage` casts and imports of the minter. |
| Corpus and eval | `scripts/world.ts`, `scripts/buildCorpus.ts`, `scripts/evalGate.ts`, `scripts/adjudicate.ts`, `scripts/summarize.ts` | See below. |

Tests: 15 pass (`bun test prototypes/poc-leak-gate/tests`), and `tsc --noEmit -p prototypes/poc-leak-gate` is clean.

## Corpus

- **Members.** Member cards come from `data/synthetic/v1`: real facets and scopes, the private disclosures and their canaries, and synthetic phone and email. Augmentation is seeded, and augmented facet ids are prefixed `aug:`. It adds:
  - a synthetic home address and an exact routine (agent_private);
  - one extra sensitive agent_private fact per adult, so health, sexuality, finance and legal topics go beyond the dataset's 8 disclosure templates;
  - a canary for the 70% of members without one;
  - for 25% of members, a *public* sensitive-sounding fact as a hard negative (for example "is sober and hosts alcohol-free meetups").
- **Scenarios.** Each scenario has a recipient, 1 or 2 co-participants, 1 non-participant and a public venue.
- **How labels are set.** For each leak, claude-sonnet-4.5 gets exactly one invisible fact and a technique, and writes a message that leaks it. For clean messages it never sees any invisible fact.
- **Size.** 2,400 messages in total:
  - Main corpus: 170 scenarios, 680 leaks and 680 clean, split by scenario into **dev** (228 + 228) and **test** (452 + 452).
  - **test2**: a second corpus built after all tuning, with a new seed and 130 scenarios (520 + 520). No prompt or rule was tuned on it.
- **Leak techniques** (held-out n): exact 120, paraphrase 158, inference 150, timing 115, canary 72 (35% lower-case and spaced), contact 89 (35% obfuscated), address 78, location/routine 61, non-participant name 79, non-participant fact 50.
- **Topics:** health, mental health, addiction, sexuality, dating, finance, relationship, grief, work, legal, location, contact, identity and canary.
- **Clean message types:** intro; intro with a public sensitive-sounding fact; intro that echoes the recipient's *own* private fact back to them; reminder with the venue street address; logistics or reschedule; follow-up; group kickoff.

## Prompt iteration (dev split only, 228 + 228)

| Prompt | Dev recall (core + LLM) | Dev FPR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 100% | 9.2% | Strict: anything not on the shareable list counted as a leak. All 21 false positives were harmless additions not on the list, such as a work neighbourhood, venue cross-streets, "hope you hit it off" or "the group". |
| **v2 (frozen)** | 99.1% | 0.0% | Asks "does it reveal something private or sensitive?" rather than "is it on the list?". Venue addresses count as logistics. A neutral plan ("quiet spot") is fine; tying it to someone's private reason is a leak. Reference codes are leaks. |
| v3 | 98.7% | 1.3% | Adds two rules: vague mentions of unnamed others ("the group") are fine, and the recipient's own facts are never leaks. It was no better on dev, so v2 stayed frozen. v3 was run on held-out data afterwards for information only. |

The deterministic layer was also fixed during the work:

- Contraction stopwords, found by a unit test.
- From dev: name collisions with place names, the ambiguous-name list, and short sensitive tokens such as "IVF".
- From test false positives, so the test split is slightly contaminated for this layer: the canary-shape regex matching "at 2410 Folsom", the name "Ashbury" inside "Haight-Ashbury", and the lexicon firing on the recipient's own "diagnosis" or "recovery from burnout".

## False-positive patterns (v2 held-out, 18 LLM holds plus 2 lexicon holds)

1. **The recipient's own sensitive facts.** This is the largest group, at 6.5% of own-echo messages. For example, "Thinking of you, Mei. I know summer was really hard" was flagged as implying the recipient's own miscarriage. Several are romance framings built on the recipient's own dating preference: "since you're open to dating, thought you two might click". Those are arguably real problems, just policy (P22) or tone problems rather than privacy leaks. v3's explicit "recipient's own facts are never leaks" rule brought the overall rate to 1.23%.
2. **"Hit it off", "good match", "see if there's a connection"** read as implying the *other* person is dating.
3. **Vague others:** "and the others", "two others", "anyone else there".
4. **Unlisted harmless details**, for example "works in startups".
5. **Lexicon** hits on the recipient's own "job search" or "layoff" when the wording differs from the stored facet.

The deterministic core had **0** false positives on held-out data.

## Recommendation

**The gate is viable as the mandatory pre-send check. It is not viable as the only line of defence for indirect leaks.** Ship this configuration:

1. **Deterministic core, always on, with blocking holds.**
   - Canaries, contact details, addresses and routines, non-participant names, and facet n-gram overlap.
   - About 100% recall on its classes with 0 false positives, in under 3 ms, at no cost.
   - Required by the M1 onboarding messages.
2. **LLM classifier, prompt v2 or v3, on every message that passes the core.**
   - Covers paraphrase, timing and most inference leaks.
   - Set to hold rather than drop, and to fail closed when the model errors or times out.
3. **Lexicon: use it to send messages to review, not to block them.**
   - It adds nothing to combined recall on top of the LLM.
   - It adds about 0.2 points of false positives, and its words are reused in its matches.
4. **`LeakCheckedMessage` brand on the only send API, as built here.**
   - Add the source-scan test to CI so that `as LeakCheckedMessage` casts and minter imports are rejected.

**Needs human review (the P31 queue):**
- Every LLM hold. At about a 2% false-positive rate on a few thousand messages a day, that is tens of reviews a day. Most are the recipient's own facts or romance framing, so they are quick to triage.
- Any message that touches dating, sexuality, legal status or addiction about a non-recipient, even when it passes. These were the weakest topics, at 95.5% to 97% recall.
- Romance framing ("you two might click", "hit it off") when the other party's opt-in is not shareable. Route these through the P22 policy judge, not the privacy gate.
- Pattern-of-life and timing signals across messages: a regular weekday unavailability, or a burst of outreach after a disclosure. Single-message classification cannot see these. They need the INV-PRIV-10 statistical check.

**Before claiming R6 is retired:**
1. Build an expert-written set of 200 or more *clear* inference and timing leaks, with human labels. The model-written set is too weak: 157 of 186 inference items were judged weak or not leaks, so it cannot measure 99.5% on that class.
2. Run k=2 self-consistency only on messages that touch sensitive topics. Holding when either of two runs flags gave 99.18% recall at 2.98% FPR overall, which is too costly to apply everywhere.
3. Pick between v2 and v3 on a fresh human-labelled set. v3's false-positive rate was better on held-out data (1.23% vs 1.85%) but not on dev.
4. Scrub or pseudonymize contact details and addresses before they reach the LLM prompt (SEC-001). The classifier does not need raw values, because the deterministic layer handles them.
5. Record model version and prompt version on every verdict. Measured run-to-run drift was ±0.5 to 1 point on FPR.

## Caveats

- **The labels come from a model.** Even with three model families, the gate may share blind spots with the generator: a leak sonnet could not write subtly enough is also one luna is not tested on. The adjudicator also disagreed with policy in places. It rated 17 non-participant-name leaks "no", while the PRD says any non-participant identity is a leak, so those labels stand. Adjudicated numbers estimate label noise; they are not ground truth.
- **The clean messages are LLM-written and fairly polite.** Real agent drafts will be longer and more varied, and the own-fact-echo class may be rarer or more common than here.
- **The visibility model is a POC mapping.** Production also needs consent state, such as contact shared after a swap or a decliner revealed only with consent.
- **The deterministic layer was tuned partly against test false positives.** test2 is the clean held-out set for it: core FPR 0/520, core recall 61.0%.

## Fixes (after review)

All before/after numbers use the **same held-out splits** (test + test2: 972 leaks, 972 clean), plus a **fresh test3 split** generated after the fixes and the prompt choice were frozen (seed 31337041, 100 scenarios, 400 leaks + 400 clean; deepseek-v4-pro judged 309 of its leaks clear, 62 weak and 29 not leaks). "Clean held" means HOLD_REVIEW or BLOCK on a clean message.

### What changed

| # | Fix | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contact details and addresses never reach the LLM prompt.** Every facet value is scrubbed before rendering. Phones, emails, street addresses, units, decimal coordinates and reference codes become `[PHONE]`, `[EMAIL]`, `[STREET_ADDRESS]`, `[STREET]`, `[UNIT]`, `[COORDINATES]` and `[REF_CODE]`. Routines keep their schedule and lose their street ("swims at the [STREET] rec center pool at 6:10am on Tuesdays and Thursdays"). Each non-recipient gets one redacted contact line. The public venue address is still shown, because it is allowed in messages. A scan of all 300 test/test2 scenario contexts found 0 raw contact values. The draft itself is not scrubbed. In `decide()`, a draft with a detectable contact detail is blocked before any LLM call. | `src/scrub.ts`, `renderGateContext(…, "c2")` |
| 2 | **Scope-aware own facts.** *LLM:* context version **c2** groups facts into three sections: the recipient's own facts (every scope, explicitly "fine to say back"), other people's shareable facts, and other people's hidden facts. Each line is labelled `[owner: X (RECIPIENT / co-participant / NOT a participant) \| visibility: …]`. *Deterministic:* the recipient's own phone (any format), email, street address, unit and canary are masked before the contact, address and canary rules run. A lexicon hit is now suppressed when its sentence is about the recipient only (second person, no other name, no third-person pronoun, no "both / too / you two") and the recipient owns a fact on that topic. The old c1 rendering is kept for replay. | `src/visibility.ts` (`relation`, `own`), `src/classifier.ts`, `src/deterministic.ts` (`maskOwn`, `subjectOf`) |
| 3 | **`decide()` returns SEND / HOLD_REVIEW / BLOCK plus reasons.** Deterministic core hit → BLOCK, with no LLM call. LLM leak → HOLD_REVIEW. LLM unparseable output, a thrown error or the deadline (`timeoutMs`, default **8 s**, covering all retries) → HOLD_REVIEW, never SEND. Lexicon → HOLD_REVIEW only. Dating, sexuality, legal or addiction about a non-recipient → HOLD_REVIEW, even when the fact is shareable; this fires on a lexicon word in a non-recipient sentence or on the classifier's new `sensitive_topics_about_others` tag. Romance framing without a recipient-visible opt-in from every co-participant → HOLD_REVIEW; this fires on explicit romance words outside recipient-only sentences, or on the classifier's new `romance_framing` tag. Every signal that fired is returned in `reasons`. Only SEND mints a `LeakCheckedMessage`. `decide()` lives in `gate.ts`, so the brand rule ("only gate.ts mints") still holds. | `src/gate.ts`, 19 offline `decide()` branch tests with a fake LLM (plus scrub and scope tests) in `tests/pipeline.test.ts` |
| 4 | **Prompt v3 is now frozen** (`CURRENT_PROMPT = "v3"`, context c2). See the choice below. | `src/classifier.ts` |
| 5 | **Cross-message pattern check (sketch).** `PatternTracker` keeps, per recipient, the last N messages (default 10, optional time window). It attributes sentences to the third party they name (pronoun sentences inherit the last name) and extracts days, parts of day, clock times, routine places and recurrence words. It flags when the union for one person **matches a recipient-invisible routine or availability facet** (`routine_match`: at least 2 of its days plus a part-of-day, time or place anchor) or **forms a recurring weekly pattern the recipient cannot see** (`recurring_pattern`). It ignores whatever that person's visible availability already explains. `crossMessage: true` means no single message revealed the pattern alone. `peek()` checks a draft before it is sent. Deterministic and in-memory; it is not wired into `decide()` and was not measured on the corpus, which has no message sequences. | `src/pattern.ts`, 11 tests in `tests/pattern.test.ts` |
| 6 | Measurement: `scripts/evalPipeline.ts` runs the real `decide()` through a request-keyed disk cache (`data/pipeline-cache/`). It can also replay the original c1 verdicts from `data/eval-cache/`. `scripts/summarizeFixes.ts` pools the results and adds Wilson CIs. The timeout is applied from each item's measured LLM latency. | |

Two deterministic bugs were fixed along the way:
- **Namesakes.** `subjectOf` treated the recipient's own first name as a third party when another directory member shared it. Fixed on dev, before the freeze.
- **Ambiguous names (after test3).** Both test3 clean BLOCKs were "Mott Haven" matching the directory member "Haven". The guard regex placed its lookbehind before the separator group, so the existing "the/a/an Haven" guard had never fired. It now also skips a name that follows a capitalized word. test3 was scored before this fix (frozen numbers below) and again after it, so **test3 is contaminated for this one rule.**

The dyadic "you two might click / hit it off" was tried as a deterministic romance signal and dropped. It is the house style of ordinary friend intros, and on test it held 37 of 452 clean messages (8.2%). Subtle romance framing is left to the classifier's tag. This rule was tuned on test data; test3 is the clean check for it.

### 4. Prompt choice: v3 (on c2)

The choice uses held-out test + test2 and two criteria: recall on adjudicated-clear leaks, and FPR.

| Prompt (context c2) | Recall | Recall, adjudicated-clear (n=759) | LLM-only clear recall | FPR (core + LLM) | Own-fact-echo FPR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 | 99.28% [98.5, 99.7] | 99.87% | 99.74% | 2.16% [1.4, 3.3] | 7.1% |
| **v3 (chosen)** | 98.87% [98.0, 99.4] | **99.87%** | 99.60% | **1.54% [0.9, 2.5]** | **3.9%** |
| dev, v2 / v3 | 98.68% / 98.68% | 100% / 100% | | 0.44% / 0.88% | 0% / 0% |

- **Clear recall is the same.** Both prompts miss only sc124-L0 ("you two might click over natural wine").
- **v3 cuts false positives by 29%** and halves own-fact-echo false positives.
- **v3 misses 4 more leaks,** all of them inference leaks the auditor judged weak or not leaks.
- Under the stated criterion, that makes **v3** the choice. Dev cannot separate the two (1 versus 2 false positives out of 228).

**Recall by technique, test3:**
- 100% on exact, paraphrase, timing, canary, contact, address, routine, non-participant name and non-participant fact.
- **Inference: 54/62 (87%)**. All 12 inference leaks the auditor judged clear were caught; the 8 misses are weak (6) or not leaks (2), all on dating, legal or health.
- The new review rules did not rescue any of the old inference misses: none of them contains a lexicon word, and the classifier did not tag them.

**Takeaways:**
- **The timeout drives reviewer load.** In these runs (16 concurrent requests) the classifier's p50 was 3.1 to 3.3 s and its p95 6.3 to 7.6 s. Between 1.4% and 4.3% of calls ran past 8 s, and each of those becomes a review. Going from 8 s to 15 s cuts the clean HOLD_REVIEW rate from 7.1% to 2.8% on test + test2, and recall does not change. Options: a 15 s deadline for asynchronous outreach, one fast retry before holding, or a deadline set from production latency. The 8 s default is configurable (`timeoutMs`).
- **Without timeouts, reviewer load is about 2.5 to 2.8% of clean traffic.** That is about 25 to 28 reviews per 1,000 messages: roughly 1.5% LLM leak calls, 1% romance framing and 0.1 to 0.2% sensitive-topic holds. The romance and topic holds are policy holds by design.
- **The target is still not met as an unattended gate.** Recall on fresh test3 is 98.0%, and every miss is a weak or no-leak inference item. Recall on clear leaks is 100% (309/309, CI lower bound 98.8%). The earlier conclusion stands: per-message classification cannot carry the subtle-inference class, and the human-labelled inference set is still needed.

## Timeout decision (2026-10-06)

The LLM deadline now depends on the send mode (`decide(..., { mode })`):
- **async** (proactive outreach; the default): 15 s. On test + test2, 15 s held 2.78% of clean traffic for review versus 7.10% at 8 s, with identical recall. Nobody is waiting on these sends.
- **live** (a reply inside a conversation): 8 s, plus a hedged second classifier request at 4 s; the first answer wins. This cuts the slow-tail timeouts that caused most of the 8 s holds without making members wait longer. A fast error still fails closed to HOLD_REVIEW.

Tests: `tests/pipeline.test.ts` covers the default, live, hedge-rescue, both-stalled and no-hedge-in-async cases (57/57 pass). Live hedging has not been re-measured on the corpus yet; do that with `scripts/evalPipeline.ts` once the gate runs on a real reply path.
